Science coverage at WeSearchAI aims to translate complex research into accurate, accessible reporting while preserving methodological nuance. We prioritise peer-reviewed studies, official research reports, and expert commentary; when covering preprints or early-stage findings we clearly label them and explain limitations and replication status.

Our articles cover a wide range of scientific domains — biomedical research, climate and environmental science, energy technology, materials science, and computational methods — with the consistent goal of helping readers evaluate evidence and understand real-world implications. We describe study designs, sample sizes, limitations, and whether results have been replicated or independently validated. When studies rely on specialized datasets or statistical models we summarise the approach and point to the original materials so readers can review the source.

We also explain how scientific advances translate into policy or commercial outcomes: for example, how a new battery technology may affect energy grids, or what a drug trial’s endpoints mean for clinical practice. Where controversy or divergent interpretations exist, our reporting presents the different viewpoints and the evidence supporting each.

If you have a paper, dataset, or expert contact to recommend for coverage, email `editorial@wesearchai.com` with the link and a brief description of why it matters. We welcome contributions from researchers and institutions but require transparent disclosures of funding and conflicts of interest.

We maintain an internal checklist for science reporting that includes verifying statistical methods, checking whether findings have been peer-reviewed, and confirming reproducibility where possible. For complex studies we consult domain experts to explain technical details and to provide independent perspectives. Our science features endeavour to bridge the gap between specialist literature and public understanding by offering plain-language summaries accompanied by citations and links to datasets, code repositories, and supplementary materials. This approach helps readers evaluate how robust findings are and where further research is needed.

In addition to short explainers, our science section publishes longer context pieces that place discoveries into historical and practical perspective. These features often include interviews with the original researchers, links to datasets and code, and independent commentary. We identify limitations in study design and note areas where replication or further investigation is required. When possible we include practical guidance for policymakers and practitioners about how findings might translate into operational changes or further trials.

Open data and reproducibility

We strongly encourage authors to make data and code available where ethical and legal constraints permit. Long-form science pieces include a reproducibility checklist and, when available, links to code repositories and datasets. We also publish methods appendices describing statistical approaches and sensitivity analyses so readers and reviewers can assess robustness. Researchers who can provide data, code, or replication materials should contact `editorial@wesearchai.com` to coordinate data-sharing and attribution.
